# Imagery

- Give each image a job. Use a real photograph for a physical subject,
  a current screenshot for an interface, or a simple schematic for
  relationships and flow.
- Place the visual next to the point it supports. Prefer landscape
  framing for articles. Crop excess space and keep the relevant labels
  and controls readable at the size readers will see.
- Favor simple, topic-focused illustrations with restrained styling.
  Use recognizable, accurate marks when the identity of a tool matters.
- Show originals and alternatives side by side at a comparable scale.
  Use small thumbnails with on-demand enlargement so multiple
  alternatives fit without taking over the page.
- Refresh documentation screenshots after a visible interface change.
  Check for distortion, clipped text, and distracting details. For web
  articles with a fallback image, prefer WebP with a matching PNG.
